## Reference quality rules

The target is **verified and unique** sources, not many arbitrary links.

1. Deduplicate by DOI, PMID, canonical URL, and normalized title.
2. Exclude retracted, withdrawn or deprecated sources from claim support.
3. Clearly identify preprints, drafts, beta docs and vendor marketing, and lower confidence pending review.
4. Prefer direct topical relevance and an appropriate kind of source.
5. Treat standards, official specifications, systematic reviews and directly relevant controlled studies/benchmarks as strong evidence when their methods support the claim.
6. Use citation counts, author reputation, stars/downloads and publisher prestige only as secondary signals when a source explicitly provides them; these signals are age- and field-biased.
7. Preserve contradictory and null evidence rather than optimizing for agreement.
8. Do not invent missing authors, venues, versions, numbers, DOIs, or conclusions.
9. Do not pad a shortfall with weak or duplicate records. Report the gap and refine the search.
10. Do not claim full-text review when only a snippet, abstract or paywalled landing page was available.

The system's source tiers (high / moderate / contextual / low) are transparent heuristics. They assist prioritization but do not replace your own appraisal.
